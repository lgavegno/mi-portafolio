# ADR-009: Schema JSON-LD Global Sin Geolocalización Restrictiva

**Status**: Accepted

**Feature**: `FEATURE-03_AEO_SCHEMA`
**Supersedes**: —
**Related**: ADR-006, ADR-008

## Context

The portfolio is a freelancer's business card for international lead generation targeting the US and EU markets. A Schema.org structured data implementation is required to signal to search engines and AI answer engines (AEOs) who Leandro Gavegno is, what he offers, and where he serves.

Three schema patterns were evaluated:

| Pattern | Description | Geographic Signal | Use Case |
|---------|-------------|---|---|
| **A — LocalBusiness** | Represents a business with a physical address. Includes `streetAddress`, `city`, etc. | ✅ **Restrictive**: declares a single physical location (Rafaela, Argentina) to search engines and AEOs. | ❌ Misaligned: freelancer with global reach, but schema signals local-only service area. |
| **B — Person + Organization (no geo)** | Two schemas: `Person` (freelancer identity) + `Organization` (business entity). No geographic restriction. `areaServed: "Worldwide"`. | ✅ **Global**: no geographic boundaries. Signals worldwide availability. | ✅ Aligned: accurately represents a distributed freelancer serving remote clients across US, EU, LATAM. |
| **C — Person Only** | Single schema describing the freelancer. No Organization context. | ✅ Global (no restriction) | ⚠️ Incomplete: does not establish business legitimacy or organizational context for B2B leads. |

A freelancer in Rafaela, Argentina using LocalBusiness schema creates a geographic fence: search engines and AI models learn to associate the business with Argentina, potentially downranking it for US and EU queries. Person + Organization with `areaServed: "Worldwide"` accurately represents the business model and removes geographic restrictions.

---

## Decision

Implement Schema.org structured data using **Person + Organization** schemas with **`areaServed: "Worldwide"`**. Completely discard LocalBusiness.

Schema structure:
```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Person",
  "name": "Leandro Gavegno",
  "jobTitle": "Full-Stack Web Developer",
  "url": "https://utveckling.dev",
  "sameAs": ["https://github.com/...", "https://linkedin.com/..."],
  "worksFor": {
    "@type": "Organization",
    "name": "Ungévag Studio",
    "url": "https://ungévag.studio",
    "areaServed": "Worldwide"
  }
}
```

- `Person` represents the freelancer's identity (name, title, links)
- `Organization` establishes business legitimacy and sets `areaServed: "Worldwide"`
- No physical address, no geographic restriction
- Schema is deployed globally (same schema for `/` and `/es`)

---

## Consequences

**Positive**:
- Removes geographic fencing: search engines and AEOs see worldwide availability, not Argentina-only
- Accurate business model representation: Person (freelancer) + Organization (business entity) is more honest and precise than LocalBusiness
- No geographic bias in search ranking: US and EU queries are not disadvantaged
- Supports international lead generation objective: schema signals global reach to search engines and AI models (GPTBot, Perplexity, etc.)
- Explicit `areaServed: "Worldwide"` is a strong semantic signal that overrides geographic inference from whois data

**Negative / Trade-offs**:
- None documented. LocalBusiness was never the correct pattern for this use case.

**Neutral**:
- Schema markup is locale-agnostic (same for `/` and `/es`); only translated fields differ (e.g., `jobTitle`)
- Organization name and URL remain constant across both language variants
- Schema is deployed as a JSON-LD `<script>` tag in the page `<head>`, parsed by crawlers at render time

---

## Alternatives Rejected

**Option A (LocalBusiness)**: LocalBusiness is designed for businesses with a physical storefront or office where customers visit in person (e.g., a restaurant, a dental practice, a retail store). A freelancer with no physical office and all clients remote should never use LocalBusiness. Doing so creates a geographic restriction that actively harms international visibility. This option is architecturally misaligned with the business model.

**Option C (Person Only)**: While technically viable (no geographic restriction), omitting Organization diminishes the perception of business legitimacy. For B2B lead generation, establishing an organizational entity (Ungévag Studio) is strategically important. Person alone is insufficient.
